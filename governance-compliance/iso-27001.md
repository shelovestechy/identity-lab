# ISO/IEC 27001 - Practical View

ISO/IEC 27001 is an international standard for information security management.

It defines requirements for an **ISMS**: Information Security Management System.

In plain words, ISMS means the organization has a structured way to manage information security, not just random security tasks here and there.

ISO/IEC 27001 is not only about IT tools.

It is about how the organization manages security with people, processes, responsibilities, risk management, controls, documentation and continuous improvement.

---

## What ISO/IEC 27001 is for

ISO/IEC 27001 helps an organization protect information in a controlled and repeatable way.

The organization should understand:

- what information needs protection
- what risks affect that information
- what controls are needed
- who owns the risks
- how controls are implemented
- how security is monitored
- how issues are improved over time

The standard is built around managing risk.

It does not say “every organization must use the exact same security setup”.

Instead, the organization needs to understand its own risks and choose suitable controls.



## What it contains

At a high level, ISO/IEC 27001 includes requirements around:

- organization context
- leadership and responsibility
- risk assessment
- risk treatment
- security objectives
- competence and awareness
- communication
- documented information
- operational control
- performance evaluation
- internal audit
- management review
- continual improvement

The standard also includes **Annex A**, which lists security controls that can be used to treat risks.

In ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Annex A has 93 controls grouped into four themes:

- organizational controls
- people controls
- physical controls
- technological controls

For IAM work, the most interesting parts are usually around access control, identity lifecycle, privileged access, supplier access, logging, monitoring, incident management and evidence.



## What it “requires” in simple words

ISO/IEC 27001 does not just ask an organization to have security tools.

It expects the organization to manage information security in a planned way.

That means the organization should be able to show things like:

- risks have been identified
- controls have been selected for a reason
- responsibilities are clear
- people know what they are supposed to do
- security processes are documented
- controls are reviewed
- problems are corrected
- improvement happens over time

The important word is **managed**.

Security should not depend only on memory, luck or one person who “just knows how things work”.



## How this connects to access work

In access management, ISO/IEC 27001 becomes practical very fast.

Access should have a reason.

Access should have an owner.

Access should be approved by the right person.

Access should be removed when it is no longer needed.

Privileged access should be limited and reviewed.

Leaver accounts should not stay active.

Temporary access should not become permanent by accident.

This is where the standard stops being abstract and starts showing up in daily work.

A group membership, an access request or a leaver ticket can all be part of information security control.

---

## My practical interpretation

For me, the practical ISO/IEC 27001 question in IAM is:

> Can the organization show that access is controlled, justified and reviewable?

If yes, the process is probably in a healthier state.

If no, the organization may still have security activity, but not proper control.

There is a difference.

A ticket being closed does not automatically mean access was well managed.

A review being completed does not automatically mean access was truly challenged.

A policy existing does not automatically mean people follow it.

That gap between “process exists” and “control actually works” is the interesting part.


## What I would check in access work

When looking at access from an ISO/IEC 27001 point of view, I would check:

- are access requests documented?
- is there a clear approval path?
- are system owners known?
- are privileged rights limited and reviewed?
- are movers checked for old access?
- are leavers disabled on time?
- are temporary accesses removed?
- do access reviews include useful context?
- can the organization prove why access was granted?
